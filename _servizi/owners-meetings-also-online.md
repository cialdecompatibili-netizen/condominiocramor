The meeting is where owners decide. Good preparation (clear documents, a precise agenda, quotes compared) makes meetings shorter and decisions stronger.

## When you need it
- The annual ordinary meeting must be called
- Works or expenses need a vote
- Many owners cannot attend in person
- You want clear minutes sent on time
- There are doubts about majorities and proxies

## What's included
- Notice with a clear agenda
- Preparation of statement, quotes and documents
- Handling of proxies and checking majorities
- Minutes drafted, recorded and sent
- Video-call meetings where rules or the owners allow
- Execution of the decisions

## What you get
Clear documents, timely communication and a dedicated contact. We help you prepare every decision for the owners' meeting, with item-by-item quotes and easy-to-read reports.

## What you should know
- **Notice**: must be sent at least five days before, by certified email, registered letter, ordinary mail or hand delivery, stating place, time and agenda (art. 66 of the implementing provisions).
- **First call**: valid with at least two thirds of the building's value and a majority of owners. Resolutions need a majority of those present representing at least 500 thousandths (art. 1136).
- **Second call**: one third of the value and one third of owners is enough. Resolutions need a majority of those present and at least one third of the value, about 334 thousandths.
- **Minutes**: must be drawn up and kept in the minutes register. Absent owners can challenge within thirty days of notification; dissenting or abstaining owners within thirty days of the resolution (art. 1137).
- **Proxies**: any owner may be represented, but with more than twenty owners a proxy cannot hold more than one fifth of owners and value.
- **Online**: videoconference is possible under the conditions set by the rules, with identification of participants and the ability to speak and vote. We check with you what is needed in your building.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Calling late or with a vague agenda: the resolution becomes challengeable.
- Putting "any other business" on the agenda and then deciding on major spending.
- Drafting the minutes days later, from memory, without checking recorded statements.
