The manager looks after common areas, collects contributions, pays suppliers and enforces the building rules. Well-organised ordinary management avoids delays, disputes and unexpected costs.

## When you need it
- The building has more than eight units and needs a manager
- Your current manager does not reply or report
- Registers, owner records and documents are missing or messy
- You want a single contact for suppliers and owners
- Service contracts (cleaning, lift, garden) need review

## What's included
- Keeping the mandatory registers (owner records, minutes, appointment, accounts)
- Collection of contributions and payment of suppliers
- Bank account in the condominium's name
- Management of service contracts and insurance
- Contact with owners, tenants and the concierge
- Monitoring compliance with the building rules

## What you get
Clear documents, timely communication and a dedicated contact. We help you prepare every decision for the owners' meeting, with item-by-item quotes and easy-to-read reports.

## What you should know
- **What the manager does by law**: carries out meeting resolutions, regulates the use of common areas, collects contributions, takes protective measures, keeps the registers and prepares the annual statement (art. 1130 of the Civil Code).
- **Dedicated bank account**: the building's money must pass through an account in the condominium's name, never the manager's personal one (art. 1129).
- **Term**: the appointment lasts one year and renews for one more. The fee must be itemised, otherwise the appointment is void.
- **Transparency at the entrance**: the manager's name, address and contact details must be displayed at the entrance or main common area.
- **Insurance**: the meeting may require professional liability insurance as a condition of appointment.
- **Urgent works**: the manager may order them without a meeting but must report to the next meeting (art. 1135).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Managing cash and invoices "from memory", with no up-to-date accounting register.
- Postponing the yearly meeting: two years without a statement is among the serious irregularities that justify removal.
- Accepting a lump sum with no list of what is included and what is paid separately.
