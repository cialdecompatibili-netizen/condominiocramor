Small building, few flats, everything handled "between neighbours". It works until there is an emergency, a defaulting owner or works to pay for. Here is when the law requires a manager and why it is worth having one even when it is not mandatory.

## When it is mandatory
A manager must be appointed when there are **more than eight owners** (art. 1129 of the Civil Code). Below that, the meeting may decide not to appoint one, but management still has to be organised: accounts, insurance, maintenance, meetings.

## If there is none and there should be
If the meeting does not act, **any owner can ask the court to appoint one**. It is slower and less controllable than a choice made by the owners.

## What it takes to be a manager
The law sets precise requirements: civil rights, no certain convictions, adequate education, **initial training** and **yearly refresher training** (art. 71-bis of the implementing provisions). When assessing a candidate you can ask to see the documents: it is your right.

## The risk of a DIY "internal manager"
- Often nobody wants to be in charge: decisions slip.
- Whoever manages is personally liable for mistakes.
- Tax, privacy, safety and accounting need specific skills.

## How to choose well
1. Ask for an **itemised** quote (see our [checklist]({{ '/en/blog/guides/condominium-manager-quote-checklist/' | relative_url }})).
2. Ask how you can consult the statement and documents during the year.
3. Check emergency availability and contact channels.
4. Ask who will follow the building and how many buildings they manage.
