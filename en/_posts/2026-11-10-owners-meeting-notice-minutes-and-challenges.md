A good meeting starts with a well-made notice.

## Notice
The notice must be given at least five days before the meeting (art. 66 of the implementing provisions) and state the agenda, place, date and time.

## Minutes
The resolutions are recorded in minutes, entered in the minutes register, which the manager communicates to the owners.

## Challenging a resolution
Resolutions contrary to the law or the building rules can be challenged within thirty days: from the date of the resolution for dissenting owners, from the notification for absent ones (art. 1137 of the Italian Civil Code).

For specific cases it is wise to consult a lawyer.

## A valid resolution in five moves
1. **Notice**: written notice at least five days before, with place, time and a clear agenda. "Any other business" is not enough to decide on major spending.
2. **Quorum**: for the first call at least two thirds of the building's value and a majority of owners; for the second one third of the value and one third of owners.
3. **Majorities**: as a rule a majority of those present and at least half the value on first call, or one third on second (art. 1136 of the Civil Code). Some decisions need higher majorities.
4. **Minutes**: record attendance, thousandths, topics, votes and statements; signed by the chair and secretary and entered in the register.
5. **Notification**: absent owners must be sent the minutes, because the deadline to challenge runs from there.

## Challenging
You can challenge within thirty days (art. 1137): from the resolution for those present who dissented or abstained, from notification for absent owners. Challenging does not suspend the resolution unless the judge orders it. Before going to court, mediation must normally be attempted, and the mediation request avoids the deadline lapsing.
