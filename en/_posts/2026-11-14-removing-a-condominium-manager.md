If the manager does not answer, does not present the accounts or decides without consulting anyone, can they be removed? Yes, in two ways. The choice depends on how serious the problem is and how much time you have.

## Route 1: removal by the owners' meeting
The manager can be removed at any time by the same majority needed for appointment: a majority of those present representing at least half of the building's value, i.e. 500 thousandths (arts. 1129 and 1136 of the Civil Code).

In practice:
1. One or more owners ask for removal to be put on the agenda (two owners representing one sixth of the building's value can request a meeting).
2. The meeting votes the removal and, in the same session, **appoints the new manager**, so there is no management gap.
3. It also resolves on the handover.

This is the fastest route when owners agree.

## Route 2: court petition
Any owner can ask the court for removal in case of serious irregularities. The law lists some, for example:
- the meeting was not called to approve the annual statement for two consecutive years;
- meeting resolutions or court orders were not carried out;
- the bank account in the condominium's name was not opened or used;
- the management created uncertainty about the accounts.

It is needed when the meeting cannot decide and the damage continues. A lawyer is advisable.

## What to do right after removal
- The new manager asks the outgoing one for **all documentation**: registers, statements, contracts, bank statements, owners' list and the status of defaulting owners.
- The outgoing manager must complete urgent tasks to avoid harm to the building.
- The new name is communicated to the bank, suppliers and owners, and the register is updated.
