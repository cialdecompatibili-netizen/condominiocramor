When an owner does not pay, the building's funds suffer and the costs fall on the others.

## The tools
- **Reminders** and formal notices, also with a repayment plan.
- **Payment order** that is immediately enforceable: the manager can request it without the meeting's authorisation (art. 63 of the implementing provisions).
- **Suspension of separable common services** after six months of arrears (art. 63).
- **Information to creditors**: the manager discloses defaulters' data to unpaid creditors who ask for it.

## The advice
Act early: a timely reminder costs less than a lawsuit.

## What the manager must do
By law the manager must start enforcement within six months of the end of the financial year in which the debt falls due, unless the meeting exempts them (art. 1129 of the Civil Code). The manager may request an immediately enforceable payment order without the meeting's authorisation (art. 63 of the implementing provisions).

## What they can and cannot do
- They may suspend use of common services capable of separate enjoyment after six months of arrears.
- They cannot block access to the home or to essential services.
- The defaulter remains an owner: they may attend the meeting and vote.
- They cannot post the defaulters list on a notice board: it is given to creditors who ask.

## Who buys a flat
A new owner is jointly liable with the seller for the contributions of the current and previous year (art. 63). Before a purchase, ask the manager for a statement of the payment position.

## Prevention costs less
Clear instalments and shared deadlines, a quick reminder and, if needed, a written repayment plan reduce arrears before they become a problem for suppliers.
