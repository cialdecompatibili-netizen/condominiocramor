When a building changes manager, the cheapest quote usually wins. But the total at the bottom says little: what matters is **what is included** and **what will be billed separately**. A low fee with many extras can cost more than a higher, complete one.

## What the law says
When appointed and renewed, the manager must itemise the fee (art. 1129 of the Italian Civil Code). If not, the appointment is void. A lump-sum quote with no breakdown is a red flag.

## Roughly how much
On quote portals, indicative estimates for a manager are around 50-70 euros per unit per year, but it varies with building size, services and city. These are market figures, not our price list: the only reliable number is a written quote for your building.

## The checklist: 10 items
1. **Annual fee** for ordinary management, with VAT shown.
2. **What ordinary management covers**: annual statement, cost allocation, calling the yearly meeting, record keeping.
3. **Number of meetings included** and the cost of extra or out-of-hours ones.
4. **Site visits** planned (how many, how often).
5. **Emergency availability**: who answers, through which channel, at what hours.
6. **Percentage on extraordinary works**: the item that weighs most. Ask if there is one and how much.
7. **Reminders to defaulting owners**: how many are free and what the next ones cost.
8. **Postage, copies and travel**: included or reimbursed?
9. **Tax paperwork** (annual certification, building bonuses, F24): included or extra?
10. **Dedicated bank account** and owners' access to the movements.

## Questions to ask before voting
- Can you send the quote with every item separate and the estimated annual total?
- Which activities are NOT included?
- How can I check the statement and documents during the year?
- Who answers if there is a leak at 10 pm?
